Selected Op-eds

Utah Housing Model is Wrong Way to Go

A new report suggests Utah’s proposed centralized homeless campus, combining shelter with involuntary treatment, is a costly and potentially coercive approach with little evidence that it will reduce homelessness. It is found permanent supportive housing with voluntary services is more effective and affordable alternative that addresses the underlying causes of homelessness.

‘A Grim March Toward Death:’ What HUD’s New Homeless Policy Looks Like on the Ground

HUD’s shift away from Housing First toward transitional housing, mandatory services, and stricter conditions on federal funding is affecting local homelessness programs. Based on interviews with administrators from 55 Continuums of Care, I show the abrupt funding changes, unclear requirements, and limited federal guidance are creating instability and could push thousands of people currently in permanent housing back into homelessness.

A Tech Bro Think Tank is Trying to Roll Back Evidence-Based Homeless Policy

The Cicero Institute has helped drive a federal shift away from Housing First toward more punitive approaches, including transitional housing, encampment enforcement, and involuntary treatment. These reforms conflict with evidence supporting Housing First and risk destabilizing homelessness programs and pushing thousands of formerly unhoused people back into homelessness.

State Governors Must Act Now to Prevent Mass Homelessness

Proposed changes to federal homelessness policy could put roughly 170,000 people receiving permanent housing assistance at risk of losing their homes. State governors should prepare emergency rental assistance and eviction protections to prevent funding disruptions from triggering a major increase in homelessness.

How Social Investment Financing Can Help Tackle Homelessness

A new report suggests social investment financing can help tackle homelessness by providing charities and housing organisations with flexible, long-term funding to acquire and improve homes for people experiencing homelessness. Combining private investment with public and charitable resources can expand affordable housing while reducing reliance on temporary accommodation and overstretched public funding.
