Teaching Philosophy, Experience, and Credentials

My teaching philosophy has three pillars: alignment, inclusion, and interaction.

The first pillar “alignment” is derived from constructive alignment theory. Constructive alignment connects each element of a module—aims, objectives, activities, and assessments—to clarify and achieve the module’s purpose. After deciding what students will learn, I can select readings, design activities, and assess performance. This ensures curriculum is based on plausible outcomes toward which student progress can be measured.

The second pillar “inclusion” recoginises our experience of the world is shaped by our social location. Lecturers inevitably bring our situated experience and their resultant assumptions into the classroom. This can reproduce inequalities if curriculum is un/intentionally designed to exclude minority students. I do my best to create an inclusive classroom by reflexively creating curriculum that reflects the experience of different students.

The third pillar “interaction” is synthesised from cognitivism and social constructionism. Interactive lectures combine traditional oration with active learning to help students develop a new skill, thus showing them how to do something before they independently do it. This demystifies analytic tasks with live demonstrations while facilitating one-on-one support for students with unique needs.

I have applied this philosophy as a Graduate Teaching Assistant at the University of Wisconsin-Madison (2011–2021) and as an Associate Lecturer at Manchester Metropolitan University (2023–2024). While in those posts, I have contributed to modules on ethnic social movements, social psychology, introduction to statistics, sociology of race and ethnicity, digital sociology, and criminology.

I earned a Postgraduate Certificate in Learning and Teaching in Higher Education from Manchester Metropolitan University and became an Advanced HE Teaching Fellow in 2025.
